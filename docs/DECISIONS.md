## 2026-11-01 · v2: independent of Hermes
- The API key comes only from `RF_API_KEY` or `~/.reelfactory/nous_api_key` (saved from the page's **Settings**). The Hermes login is no longer read.

## 2026-11-01 · v2: auto-start and one-click update
- "Turn on auto-start.command" installs a LaunchAgent (com.reelfactory.dashboard) that runs scripts/autostart.sh at login (git pull --ff-only, then the dashboard). It records the Terminal PATH so node/npx/ffmpeg are found. "Turn off auto-start.command" removes it.

## 2026-11-02 — IT agent (watchdog), v1
Hesham asked for an IT agent that detects issues, budget problems and errors and acts at once, with rules so it can't loop or be abused.
Decision: v1 is a plain program inside the dashboard (`dashboard/it_agent.py`), **not an AI**: free, no internet, no keys, and it cannot be talked into anything by text in a log. It reports every problem in plain words on the dashboard and can do ONE safe action (Try again on a stuck reel), bounded to 2 tries per reel, 15 min apart, 3 per hour. ## 2026-11-02 — Stage 1 (media ingestion) upgrade

## 2026-11-02 — Stage 2 (preparing the video) upgrade
Hesham asked for a stronger Stage 2. Built, all free and local (ffmpeg only): (1) the Stage 1 media check is now really wired into `scripts/ingest_folder.py` (it was written but not connected before): every file is checked first, bad files and exact copies are skipped, `media_manifest.json` is written; (2) clips are prepared two at a time and each clip's audio, pauses and loudness are taken from the ORIGINAL at the same time as its proxy is made; (3) timestamps are normalised: constant 30 fps from time 0, sound aligned (aresample async), a keyframe every second (frame-accurate cutting), and a silent clip gets a silent sound track so clips can be joined; (4) `jobs/<job>/media_index.json` is a virtual timeline: each clip stays separate with asset id, original path, source fps/timecode, proxy, position on the joined timeline, role, speech segments, scenes and thumbnails (`jobs/<job>/thumbs/`), plus `source_to_timeline` / `timeline_to_source` in `scripts/lib/media_index.py`. The joined `proxy.mp4` is still made because the pipeline reads it, but it is no longer the only truth.
Not built (needs real footage or a choice from Hesham): matching a separate microphone recording to the camera by waveform; noise reduction / speech isolation (needs an A/B test on his real clips so it cannot make the voice worse); reusing prepared proxies across reels.

## 2026-11-02 — Stage 3 (listening to the words) upgrade
Hesham asked for speech intelligence. Built free and local (`scripts/lib/speech_intel.py`, no AI, no internet), runs after the word-fix step and writes `jobs/<job>/speech_intel.json` (also printed in run.log and added to report.md): language mix (Arabic / English / Kurdish letters, with a warning when Kurdish is heard but the transcriber is set to Arabic); glossary words used and unfixed spellings; fillers, repeated words, restarts, pauses, speaking speed, maybe-unfinished sentences; loudness per word and emphasis words; and FOUR separate scores (transcription confidence, timestamp reliability, audio quality, delivery energy), each with a plain "means" line, plus a list of low-confidence words to check. Scores are rough guides: whisper gives no confidence of its own here, so confidence comes from checkable evidence (odd word length, spelling fixer changed it, barely louder than background, single letter). The raw transcript (`transcript_asr.json`) is never changed; corrections are listed next to it.
Not built: speaker labels (needs a speaker model; one speaker is assumed and said so in the file), meaning-based importance (stays with the AI story step; the score is null on purpose), Kurdish Sorani transcription itself (whisper hears it badly: needs a different engine, Hesham's call), acoustic excitement beyond loudness/pace (pitch).

## 2026-11-02 — Stage 4 (story and cutting) upgrade
Hesham asked for a stronger editorial engine. Built: the existing story-pick AI call (same call, no extra call, answer is a little longer) now also returns the main message, the audience, a style per candidate (educational / fast / storytelling), 1-10 scores for relevance, clarity, delivery, novelty and narrative fit, and THREE alternative opening hooks per candidate (curiosity, problem, direct value). Code (`scripts/lib/editorial.py`, free) computes the story score with the proposed formula S = 0.30R + 0.25C + 0.20D + 0.15N + 0.10F (weights in `config/rules.json -> story_score`, re-scaled to add up to 1; proposed starting values, not proven predictors); delivery D is averaged with a delivery score measured from the real audio (`speech_intel.json`); every hook is checked to be word for word in the transcript (rule 5: his own words); ranges are checked for length and for not starting/ending inside a word. The director gets the main message, audience, style and hook candidates as a brief. After the plan, `jobs/<job>/editorial.json` stores the decisions as plain instructions: chosen story and source ranges, alternative stories with their score breakdown, hooks, rationale and confidence flags (tight cuts, cuts between words spoken back to back, hooks not verbatim, odd lengths); it is also added to report.md. Non-destructive: the timeline and editorial file can be changed and rebuilt, originals are never touched.
Not built (cost / needs Hesham's OK): full alternative drafts in different styles (each one is a whole extra plan and render, about the cost of another reel); pacing presets that change how tight the cuts are per style; a second AI pass that re-reads the cut for meaning. The three candidate stories and the three hooks are the alternatives for now.

## 2026-11-02 — T98 quick re-render built (Hesham said yes)
`scripts/lib/segcache.py` + `render.draft_render`. DRAFTS only (the final is always a whole render). First draft: one whole render with a keyframe every 2 s, cut without re-encoding into 6 s pieces in `jobs/<job>/render_cache/` (+ `pieces.json`). After an edit: each piece has a fingerprint (the clips/cards overlapping it, their template files, only the captions shown in it, the footage, the render recipe; anything not understood changes all fingerprints), only pieces whose fingerprint changed are rendered again (`--start/--end`), the rest are reused. If 60% or more changed, or anything fails or looks wrong (frame count off by more than 1, join error), it makes the whole draft instead: the cache can cost time but never show an old picture. Join = picture copied (raw H.264 re-timed to even 30 fps) + each piece's sound trimmed/padded to its exact length, so no drift. Proven on a real render here: 21 s reel, caption changed: 1 of 4 pieces re-rendered, 44 s instead of 64 s, 630 frames and 21.000 s of sound exactly; the real run also caught a 70 s timeline bug in the first join method (fixed, tested). Bigger savings on longer reels (about 35 s start-up per piece). Pieces can differ from a whole render by about one frame at card edges and by 1-2 grey levels (Chrome is not bit-identical between runs). The cache is never deleted automatically (AGENTS rule: nothing in jobs/ is deleted); it can grow about 40 MB per full re-seed.
